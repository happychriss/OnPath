# OnPath 0.11.3 – bike and hiking map that reads at a glance

**Bike map (Bike, Gravel bike, E-bike)**
- Same logic as for hiking: a **green line** marks a good bike way (cycle paths, bike lanes, quiet bike streets); a
  **soft mint band** marks a signed bike route, with its **name** along it; long-distance routes (EuroVelo, national
  routes) get the **pink-green band** like major hiking trails.
- Only real signed bike networks are shown as routes – some odd ways that were marked before are gone.
- Forest and field tracks are light lanes, forest paths thin dark lines; loose ground is a fine dotted edge, only when
  you zoom in close.
- One-way arrows: a **dark arrow** = one way for everyone, a **green ⟷** = one way for cars, bikes both ways, a green
  single arrow = one way only for bikes.

**Zooming out (bike and hiking)**
- Further out the map gets calm: signed routes become thin lines, paths fade away (hiking keeps them faint), so the
  route network stands out.

**Towns**
- Town streets and pedestrian streets have a clear edge again; footpaths end at the street instead of running over it.
- Squares, traffic islands and platforms are drawn as light areas; sidewalks and crossings as thin outlines when you
  zoom in close – junctions read like real places.
- Underpasses (for example under the railway at a station) are a clear path line; passages through buildings stay
  faint.
- Ways you may not use are marked more calmly; main roads keep a thin edge where a route band runs along them.

**Maps refresh faster**
- Your map areas are rebuilt once for the new look – this now happens much faster, and the app notices finished areas
  within a minute.
